BRIEF

What the app does

Each round parks a four-seat high-wing trainer on the ramp with a flight clipped to your board, and hides up to four defects on the airplane or in its paperwork. Walk around it in 3D, drain fuel samples, check the documents desk, then sign it off as go or no-go. The examiner's debrief shows SAT or UNSAT, what you missed, your false alarms and the regulation behind each ruling.

SQUAWK 01

I grounded the airplane and still got UNSAT

Some airplanes have nothing wrong with them. Grounding a good airplane counts against you, and so does calling a placard item a no-go. Whether a defect grounds the airplane depends on the flight on your clipboard: a dead position light is fine for a day flight and a no-go after sunset.

SQUAWK 02

How do I give a student the same airplane?

Open MENU > Instructor code. The short code recreates the same airplane, defects and conditions on another device. You pass the code on yourself; nothing goes through a server.

SQUAWK 03

I can't see anything at night

On night rounds you have a flashlight. Point your finger at the part you want to light up; cracked lenses and oil streaks only show where the beam lands.

SQUAWK 04

Which airplane is it, and can I rely on it?

A generic four-seat high-wing trainer, not a specific make or model. Rulings cite 14 CFR Part 91 and the Private Pilot ACS, but the app is a training aid only. Use your airplane's POH/AFM and equipment list and follow your flight instructor. Not affiliated with the FAA or any manufacturer.
